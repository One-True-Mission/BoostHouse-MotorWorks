Hands On
Our Philosophy

The motor is only as good as the hands that built it.

Jose was 18 years old when he blew the motor in his STI for the second time. Subaru had rebuilt it once. He wasn't going to let them do it again. So he tore down a $40,000 car in his parents' garage and rebuilt the motor himself.

That decision is the reason BoostHouse exists.

What started as one stubborn kid refusing to settle turned into a decade of self-taught craftsmanship. No formal training. No mentors handing him shortcuts. Just YouTube, tolerances, mistakes, and the kind of obsessive curiosity that turns a hobby into a profession. By the time he was running his own shop out of a small space in Taylor, he was already the guy other Subaru owners called when something didn't feel right.

BoostHouse came next, when Jose wanted to move closer to Austin and build something bigger. The name is four years old. The craft behind it is a lot older than that.

The way Jose approaches a motor hasn't changed since he was that 18-year-old in his parents' driveway. Tear it down. Measure everything. Figure out what actually went wrong, not just what's easiest to replace. Build it back better than it came from the factory. That's not a marketing line. That's the whole reason he's still doing this ten years in.
